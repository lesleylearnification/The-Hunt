THE CHALLENGE

Cyber-defenders try to stop attackers who often have more time, expertise, and persistence. Get it wrong, and the consequences are dire. Attacks can shut down operations, make money disappear, expose or hold sensitive data hostage, and create serious business impact.

Traditional cybersecurity training typically tells learners which controls prevent attacks. Instead, I thought it more interesting to let the learner play as the hacker. By putting cyber-warriors in the shoes of the enemy, it lets them experience which defensive weaknesses make an attack possible, and improves their ability to spot potential attacks in the wild.

LEARNING OBJECTIVE

Given a simulated cyberattack, the learner will select the defensive activities needed to interrupt the attack chain before Stage 5 Business Impact.

GAME FANTASY

Hmm…this target looks juicy. I just have to make sure none of it looks coordinated. So far, so good. All’s quiet on the Huntress front. An aggressive will get me closer to my objective…and leave a much bigger trail. Too big, and BAM!...I’m busted.

You are a hacker. And a good one! And if you can exploit the target's defensive weaknesses without allowing your activities to be correlated, your breach will be successful. But if Huntress connects the dots, you lose.

GAME GOAL

Exploit an organization's weaknesses and progress through five stages of an attack before Huntress catches you.

GAMEPLAY

Every time you hit START, you are randomly assigned one of six different targets, objectives, attack paths, defensive weaknesses, and potential business consequences.

Your job is to exploit the organization's weaknesses and reach Stage 5 before Huntress figures out what you are doing. Each move creates a tradeoff. An aggressive attack brings you closer to your objective, but it leaves a larger trail. And once your activities are correlated, Huntress begins hunting.

MOTIVATION

Autonomy: You decide how to attack, which weaknesses to exploit, how much detection risk to accept, and how aggressively to move toward your objective.

Competence/Mastery: Repeated runs teach you which defenses make attacks harder, which actions generate detectable traces, and how an attack progresses from an apparently minor vulnerability to actual business impact.

Immersion: You're not clicking NEXT, NEXT, NEXT through a barrage of cybersecurity questions. You're in the attacker's chair with a very competent opponent on your tail.

Relatedness: There are humans on both sides. You're attacking an organization populated by people, while Huntress functions as the unseen human ally protecting them.

CORE DRIVES

Curiosity: Can I get through?

Competence: Can I get better at this?

Power: exploit a system that initially appears secure.

Race to the Finish: it’s you and Huntress in direct opposition:

GAME INSPIRATION

Her Story was useful because its story isn't handed to you in order. You search through disconnected pieces of evidence and gradually realize how they fit together. That helped inspire the idea that seemingly unrelated security traces can become meaningful only when viewed as part of a larger pattern.

Return of the Obra Dinn influenced the reconstruction mechanic. It trusts the player to infer what happened from incomplete evidence instead of simply telling them the answer. THE HUNT similarly asks the player to understand how individual actions become an attack chain.

I'm on Observation Duty helped with the tension between ordinary activity and meaningful anomalies. The interesting challenge isn't merely noticing something unusual. It's deciding whether what you're seeing actually matters before the situation gets worse.

Orwell was useful for thinking about information triage. Players encounter information from different sources and have to decide which pieces are meaningful. That maps nicely to Huntress's ability to make sense of security signals that might otherwise appear disconnected.

Pandemic influenced the five-stage progression. A problem that initially looks manageable can propagate through an interconnected system until suddenly you're dealing with consequences rather than warning signs.

REFLECTION

At the end of the game, if you succeed, you find out why. If you fail, that’s explained as well. The game reconstructs the evidence your actions created and shows how those seemingly unrelated traces became a recognizable attack. You find out why you left a trail, which will help to spot them on the job. The goal is for the player to leave with a better understanding of why defensive controls matter and how an attacker's individual actions can become detectable when they are connected.
